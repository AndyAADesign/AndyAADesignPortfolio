Xapontic

Designing an e-commerce experience that balanced product discovery, cultural storytelling and an approachable ordering flow.

01 · Context

The challenge

Xapontic produces artisanal soaps through the work of Tseltal women in northern Chiapas. When our university team began the project, the organization did not have its own website. We were asked to create both a customer-facing experience and a backend that could be maintained without editing code.

The central UX question: how do we communicate the women, community and origin behind the product without making it harder to browse and order?

My ownership centered on the front-end experience: wireframes, interactive prototypes, visual UI, responsive layouts, usability testing and HTML/CSS/JavaScript implementation. Backend and database work were primarily owned by another teammate.

02 · Information architecture

Breadth first, depth second

After the client brief, we used card sorting to group the site’s major content: story, community, products, events, news, contact and ordering. That work informed a primary navigation of Inicio → Nosotras → Productos → Noticias → Contacto, with Realizar pedido emphasized as a separate commerce action.

The homepage gives breadth. Inner pages give depth.

We intentionally surfaced story before commerce because understanding the organization was part of understanding the product. The homepage acted as an overview with entry points into the brand, products, bazaars, partners and contact information.

03 · Commerce flow

Two ways into the same order

We designed for both goal-oriented visitors and browsers. Someone who already knew what they wanted could enter through the persistent order CTA; someone exploring first could move from the product catalog into the same flow.

The final order experience was divided into stages so optional decisions did not compete with the main task: choose products and quantities, decide whether to customize, enter personal information, review the order and submit.

04 · Usability testing

Testing for findability

We tested at several milestones, generally with approximately 15–20 participants per round. Tasks focused on behavior rather than preference: locate products, find an event, understand the organization, identify ingredients, contact the brand and complete an order.

One recurring area of friction was product size. Exact gram weights were necessary, but relying on numbers alone made relative sizes less immediately scannable. Today I would keep the precise weight and add stronger visual comparison cues.

05 · Responsive design

Mobile first changed the hierarchy

Early in the project, compressing large desktop layouts into mobile became difficult. We changed our workflow to Mobile → Tablet → Desktop. Starting on the smallest screen forced clearer prioritization, then larger layouts could redistribute information instead of squeezing it down.

06 · Outcome & reflection

What shipped — and what I learned

We completed and presented a functional responsive front end. The backend/CMS was less robust than we intended, and another student team’s proposal was selected by the client. That became an important production lesson: interface quality is only one part of delivering a successful product.

After the semester, our project received a Mención Honorífica in Diseña México 2020, Sitios web · Área Estudiante. The recognition highlighted the project’s grounding in client and user needs, evaluation with users and representation of the community, production process and online ordering.

What I would improve today

I would strengthen button affordance, improve product-information hierarchy, make size comparisons more visual and explore more ambitious interaction patterns before constraining the design around implementation anxiety. I would also identify technical risks earlier and distribute critical production knowledge across the team.
